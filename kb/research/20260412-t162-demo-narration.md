# Demo Narration Script: `mo:os` Menno Presentation (T-162)

## Target Audience: Chemists / Research Scientists
**Theme:** Moving from "Siloed Information" to "Federated Knowledge Operads"

---

## 1. The Opening (30 Seconds)
"Hello everyone. Today we’re looking at `mo:os`—not as a database, but as a living knowledge federation. For a chemist, information is usually trapped in PDFs, spreadsheets, or proprietary databases. `mo:os` treats every CAS number, every IUPAC name, and every experimental result as a node in a live hypergraph. It doesn't just store data; it maps the relationships between classifications in real-time."

---

## 2. Panel-by-Panel Walkthrough

### Panel 1: Federation Topology
**Explainer:** This is the 'neural map' of our network. It shows every active research node and kernel currently contributing to the federation, visualizing how they are linked across different workstations and laboratories.

**Narration:** 
"In the top-left, we see the **Federation Topology**. Think of this as the 'lab bench' of the future. Each node you see represents a piece of knowledge—perhaps a specific molecule or a research paper—and each line represents a validated relationship like 'is-isomer-of' or 'classified-by'. What’s unique here is that this graph isn't coming from one computer; it’s being pulled live from a federation of kernels on this laptop and our high-performance workstation (Z440)."

### Panel 2: Classification Space (HDC)
**Explainer:** This panel visualizes how different scientific naming systems (like CAS numbers vs. IUPAC names) relate to each other mathematically. It uses high-dimensional vectors to measure how 'close' or 'accurate' one classification system is when compared to another.

**Narration:**
"In the top-right, we have the **Classification Space**. As chemists, we deal with naming conflicts every day—a common name is not a CAS number, which is not an IUPAC string. Here, we use Hyperdimensional Computing (HDC) to project these complex nomenclature systems into a 2D map. The arrows you see are 'Crosswalks'. They mathematically verify the residual error between, say, an IFRS financial classification and a scientific arXiv metadata scheme, ensuring our data 'topology' stays consistent as it moves between domains."

### Panel 3: Temporal DAG
**Explainer:** The Temporal Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) tracks the history and planned future of our research project. It ensures that every piece of data has a clear provenance and an expected 'landing day' in our development timeline.

**Narration:**
"Bottom-left is our **Temporal DAG**. Science is a process. Here we track our progress from the initial 'Foundation' at T-159 to our planned 'Data Collider' at T-180. Each milestone is a persistent event in the kernel. Green nodes are completed discoveries; amber ones, like our current 'Menno Demo' at T-162, are currently in-progress."

### Panel 4: Value Attribution
**Explainer:** This panel acts as a 'Spectrometer for Knowledge.' it calculates the relative worth or 'contribution' of different data types (like users, kernels, or schemes) to the overall strength of the federation.

**Narration:**
"Finally, the **Value Attribution** panel. We use a 'Shapley Proxy'—a concept from game theory—to weigh the spectral density of our graph. It identifies which parts of our federation are providing the most value. For example, it might show that our 'Classification Schemes' are contributing 45% of our system's semantic coherence, helping us decide where to focus our next round of data seeding."

---

## 3. The Closing
"This is just the beginning. By T-169, we will have a live landing page for public interaction, and by T-180, we move into the 'Data Collider' phase—where these hypergraphs will begin autonomous discovery. We’re moving the laboratory out of the silo and into the federation."

## 4. Chemistry Tie-in Example
- **The Node:** `urn:moos:tag:cas:64-17-5` (Ethanol)
- **The Scheme:** `urn:moos:scheme:iupac`
- **The Crosswalk:** The system automatically verifies if the vector for "Ethanol" in a common-name scheme aligns perfectly with the "64-17-5" vector in the CAS scheme via a WF12 Interaction Node.
